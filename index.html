Income Tax Act

Income Tax Audit (Section 44AB)

Turnover ceilings, cash-transaction limits, and report forms for tax audit, along with a quick eligibility estimator.

Due date: 31 October
Business
₹10 Cr

Standard business turnover ceiling. Applies when cash transactions (receipts or payments) are 5% or less of gross turnover. Audit is compulsory beyond this mark.

Cash receipts & payments
≤ 5%

Cash receipts & payments must be ≤ 5%. Introduced to stimulate digital operations. All banking transfers, NEFT, RTGS, and UPI qualify towards non-cash percentage.

Professionals
₹50 Lakhs

Legal, Medical, Engineering, Accountancy. Standard limit is ₹50 Lakhs. Enhanced to ₹75 Lakhs under Finance Act if aggregate cash receipts do not exceed 5% of total gross receipts.

Statutory Reporting Forms Demystified

Form 3CA Already audited under another law

Furnished when the assessee is already required to get accounts audited under any other statute (e.g. Companies Act 2013). The auditor merely annexes Form 3CD with qualifying notes.

Form 3CB Not otherwise audited

Furnished when the taxpayer is an individual, HUF, or partnership firm whose accounts are not mandatorily audited under any other legislation prior to Section 44AB.

Clause 44 Disclosure Note:
Form 3CD must accompany either report and forms part of the tax audit report filed with the income tax return.

GST Annual Return & Reconciliation

GSTR-9 & 9C Reconciliation

Annual return and reconciliation requirements by turnover tier, plus the four-step pipeline to reconcile your books with GST filings.

Due date: 31 December
UP TO ₹2 CR
GSTR-9: Exempt from mandatory filing

Registered taxpayers with aggregate PAN-level turnover under ₹2 Crores are exempted from mandatory filing of GSTR-9. Voluntary filing is advised to freeze ITC disputes.

₹2 CR – ₹5 CR
GSTR-9 mandatory, GSTR-9C not required

Form GSTR-9 Annual Return is mandatory. Detailed outward supplies, inward supplies under RCM, and tax paid must be reconciled. GSTR-9C reconciliation statement is exempt.

ABOVE ₹5 CR
GSTR-9 & GSTR-9C mandatory

Both GSTR-9 (Annual Return) and GSTR-9C (Reconciliation Statement) are strictly mandatory. Taxpayers self-certify turnover and tax reconciliations directly on the GST Common Portal.

The 4-Step Institutional GST Reconciliation Pipeline

PHASE 01

Audited Books of Account

Extract audited P&L balance, gross turnover, inter-state stock transfers, other incomes, and advances received.

PHASE 02

Turnover Alignment

Reconcile differences between GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, and Balance Sheet gross revenues to pinpoint unbilled revenue or timing adjustments.

PHASE 03

ITC 2B vs GSTR-3B

Execute strict invoice-matching against auto-populated GSTR-2B. Flag uncredited invoices, Section 17(5) blocked credits, and rule 37 reversals.

PHASE 04

Form DRC-03 Clearance

Discharge additional tax, interest, or excess ITC reversal through DRC-03 voluntary payment mode prior to filing confirmation.

Institute of Chartered Accountants of India

ICAI Standards on Auditing (SAs) Codex

The core Standards on Auditing that govern planning, risk assessment, audit evidence, and audit opinions.

Ind AS aligned financial reporting
SA 200

Overall Objectives

Governs reasonable assurance, professional skepticism, ethical requirements, and auditor independence in financial statement reviews.

SA 315 & SA 320

Risk & Materiality

Identifying risks of material misstatement through understanding entity controls and establishing materiality thresholds for audit scope.

SA 500 & SA 505

Audit Evidence

Sufficient and appropriate evidence design, sampling routines, external confirmations from banks, debtors, and active legal counsels.

SA 700, 705 & 706

Opinions

Framework for formulating Unmodified, Qualified, Adverse, or Disclaimer of Opinion, plus Emphasis of Matter paragraphs.

The 4 Audit Opinion Classifications (SA 700 & 705)

01. Unmodified (Clean)

Financial statements give a true and fair view in all material respects in line with AS / Ind-AS.

02. Qualified Opinion

Misstatements are material but not pervasive to the financial statements taken as a whole.

03. Adverse Opinion

Misstatements are both material AND pervasive, distorting the entire economic depiction.

04. Disclaimer of Opinion

Auditor unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence; potential effects are both material and pervasive.
